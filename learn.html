Calendar Trick

Find the weekday of any date

    The codes

    These are the only things you have to memorize. Cover the answers to test yourself, then tap a hidden code to check it.

    Weekdays

    SunMonTueWedThuFriSat
    0123456

    Sunday is 0, and the rest follow in order. A total of 7 is Sunday again.

    Months

    Q1Jan 6Feb 2Mar 2
    Q2Apr 5May 0Jun 3
    Q3Jul 5Aug 1Sep 4
    Q4Oct 6Nov 2Dec 4

    Read them as 622 · 503 · 514 · 624. Q1 and Q4 both start with 62. Q2 and Q3 both start with 5, and Q3 is Q2 plus one in the last two digits.

    In a leap year, January and February drop by 1: January 5, February 1.

    Centuries

    1600s2000s0
    1700s2100s5
    1800s2200s3
    1900s2300s1

    The pattern 0 5 3 1 repeats every 400 years. It's the same start as the leap-year codes, because each century also moves the weekday by 5.

    Leap years within a century

    00040812162024
    0531642

    Each step subtracts 2 and wraps around 7: 0 5 3 1 6 4 2. After 24 the cycle starts again at 28. That's why you reduce the year mod 28 first.

    Mod 28 shortcut

    28 to 55subtract 30, add 2
    56 to 83subtract 60, add 4
    84 to 99subtract 90, add 6

    Example: 73 − 60 + 4 = 17. Then 17 is 16 (code 6) plus 1 year, so its year code is 7, which is 0.

    Why it works

    Every code counts how far a date shifts the weekday. A normal year is 365 days, which is 52 weeks and 1 day. So each extra year adds 1. Four years add 4 plus a leap day, which is 5. That's the step in the leap-year table.

    Leap years are divisible by 4. The exception is century years, which are leap years only when divisible by 400. So 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 was not.
